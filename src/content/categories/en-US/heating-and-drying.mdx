Heating and drying equipment helps commercial teams control temperature, moisture, and humidity during construction, restoration, finishing, and temporary occupancy. The category includes indirect and direct fired heaters, electric heaters, dehumidifiers, air movers, and drying accessories. Renting lets contractors scale climate control to the building envelope, season, material sensitivity, and project schedule. Good rental planning considers the volume of space, ventilation, fuel availability, moisture source, target conditions, and whether people will occupy nearby areas during operation.

## Common Use Cases

- Temporary heat for winter construction, curing, and interior fit-out.
- Drying after water intrusion, concrete work, drywall finishing, or coatings.
- Humidity control for materials, storage areas, and sensitive finishes.
- Spot heating for work zones, tents, warehouses, and temporary structures.
- Air movement support for restoration, ventilation, and drying workflows.

## What to Consider Before Renting

- Estimate area volume, insulation level, outdoor temperature, and target temperature.
- Choose electric, direct-fired, or indirect-fired heat based on ventilation and occupancy.
- For drying, identify moisture source, humidity target, drainage, and air movement needs.
- Plan fuel supply, power circuits, ducting, condensate handling, and safe equipment placement.
- Monitor site conditions because drying performance changes with envelope and weather.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between direct and indirect fired heat?

Direct-fired heaters discharge combustion products into the heated air, while indirect-fired units keep combustion exhaust separate through a heat exchanger.

### Do I need heat, drying, or both?

Heat raises temperature, while drying often needs dehumidification and air movement. Many projects use a coordinated package.

### Can drying equipment speed up concrete or coating schedules?

It can help create suitable conditions, but material manufacturer requirements and site monitoring should guide timing.

### What details help size temporary heat?

Space dimensions, insulation, target temperature, outside temperature, fuel access, ventilation, and occupancy are the key inputs.
