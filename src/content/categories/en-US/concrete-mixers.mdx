Concrete mixers are rented for on-site batching when crews need controlled quantities of concrete or mortar close to the work area. They are common on repair jobs, small slabs, footings, fence and bollard installations, curb work, and projects where ready-mix delivery is not practical for every pour. Rental mixers help contractors adjust capacity to the volume and pace of the job. The right choice depends on batch size, material type, power source, loading method, washout plan, and how far the mix must be moved after discharge.

## Common Use Cases

- Small concrete pours for pads, repairs, curbs, and equipment bases.
- Masonry mortar mixing for commercial renovation and site work.
- Utility, landscape, and hardscape installations with distributed pour locations.
- Remote or phased work where ready-mix trucks are inefficient.
- Backup mixing capacity for patching and punch-list concrete work.

## What to Consider Before Renting

- Estimate total volume and batch frequency so the mixer keeps pace with the crew.
- Choose drum size, towable format, or portable style based on access and material handling.
- Confirm electric or gas power, ventilation, and generator needs.
- Plan water access, aggregate loading, discharge height, and washout containment.
- Clarify whether stands, wheelbarrows, tubs, or cleanup accessories are required.

## FAQ

### Can I mix concrete and mortar in the same mixer?

Some mixers are better for concrete aggregate, while mortar mixers use paddle action for masonry materials. Specify the material before renting.

### Do mixers include water tanks?

Not always. Many jobs need a separate water source and a washout plan that complies with site rules.

### How do I choose mixer size?

Use expected batch size, crew pace, and access constraints. A larger mixer is not helpful if material loading or discharge slows the crew.

### Can a mixer be used inside a building?

Electric units may be suitable in some indoor settings, but access, ventilation, spills, and washout must be planned carefully.
