Breakers and hammers are used to fracture concrete, asphalt, rock, masonry, and compacted materials during demolition or site preparation. The category includes hand-held electric or pneumatic hammers, hydraulic breakers for carriers, and related demolition attachments. Renting lets contractors match impact energy and carrier compatibility to the material being removed without keeping every hammer class in fleet. For B2B buyers, the key is balancing productivity with control, noise, vibration, access, and the risk of damaging nearby structures or utilities.

For a clearer RFQ, describe whether the work is selective removal, continuous production breaking, or support for another machine on site. That context helps suppliers understand whether mobility, impact energy, accessory tooling, or carrier pairing should drive the recommendation.

## Common Use Cases

- Breaking concrete slabs, foundations, curbs, and pavement sections.
- Utility trench openings and localized asphalt removal.
- Masonry demolition, wall openings, and structural alteration work.
- Rock trimming, oversized material reduction, and trench support tasks.
- Pairing hydraulic breakers with excavators or skid steers for heavier demolition.

## What to Consider Before Renting

- Match impact class to material thickness, reinforcement, and production target.
- Confirm whether electric, pneumatic, or hydraulic power is available on site.
- Check carrier weight, hydraulic flow, mounting plate, and tool compatibility for attachments.
- Plan dust, noise, vibration, debris handling, and nearby occupied-space constraints.
- Include points, chisels, hoses, compressors, or power supply needs in the quote request.

## FAQ

### Do I need a compressor for a breaker rental?

Pneumatic breakers need a compressor sized for CFM and pressure demand. Electric and hydraulic options use different power sources.

### Can a breaker fit any excavator?

No. Hydraulic flow, operating weight, mounting configuration, and coupler type must be compatible with the carrier.

### Are bits and points included?

Tooling varies by supplier. Specify the material and the tool style needed so wear items and accessories are quoted correctly.

### How should reinforced concrete be described in an RFQ?

Mention slab thickness, reinforcement, access, disposal plan, and whether the work is inside or outside.
